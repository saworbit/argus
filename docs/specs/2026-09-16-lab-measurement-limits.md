## The detection limit

`sigma` is the pooled within-arm standard deviation across every
committed same-build arm on that map: one build, one map, so the
spread is the instrument and nothing else. The MDE columns are the
smallest difference in arm means detectable at that many tapes a
side, at 5 per cent false positives and 80 per cent power. Tapes
enter as a square root, so halving the detectable effect costs four
times the tapes.

## What several gates cost

## What the stopping rule does on a null

A same-build arm of four or more tapes, split in half: two arms that
differ by nothing but the match. 10 splits, 58 decisions across the
metrics with a measured sigma.

| call | count | share |
|---|---|---|
| accept (must be none) | 0 | 0% |
| reject | 19 | 33% |
| continue or abandon | 39 | 67% |

Fed one tape at a time, which is how a ladder is actually run: 19 of
58 nulls reached a decision, at a mean of 1.9 tapes a side, and 39
did not. READ THE CEILING BEFORE THE MEAN: these splits offer a
median of 2 tapes a side, because most committed arms are four or
five tapes long. The undecided rows are mostly arms that ran out of
tapes, and the mean is conditional on deciding at all, so it says the
ones that decided decided early - not that a null costs two tapes.

The continue rows are the point. At three to five tapes a side this
instrument frequently cannot rule an effect out either, and saying
so is the answer the lab has never been able to give: the old rule
had to return improved, regressed or parity whatever the evidence
was.

The any-gate row is the number that matters and it is always the
largest one in the table. Four gates each with their own chance to
convict is not one test. That is why `compare_band` takes a
pre-registered primary metric: name the gate the change is expected
to move BEFORE the tapes are in, and the rest flag without
convicting. Deciding which gate to believe after seeing the tapes is
how a coin flip gets written down as a finding.
